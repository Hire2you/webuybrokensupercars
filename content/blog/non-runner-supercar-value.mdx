A supercar that will not start is still a supercar. The reg alone tells a specialist the model year, engine, trim and likely spec — all of which feed into value.

## Scrap weight vs specialist value

A scrap merchant pays by the tonne. A 2.2-tonne Bentley Continental and a 1.5-tonne Porsche 911 might receive similar scrap offers despite very different parts values.

Specialist buyers price the whole vehicle: desirable parts, complete interior, wheels and tyres, catalytic converter, and demand for that exact model on the breaker market.

## What affects your offer

- **Model and year** — Ferrari, Lamborghini, Porsche and McLaren models have strong parts networks
- **Mileage** — lower mileage engines and gearboxes command more even when faulty
- **Trim and options** — higher spec can mean more valuable components
- **Fault type** — some failures are more costly to repair than others, but parts may still be excellent
- **Completeness** — missing wheels, interior stripped or major accident damage will reduce value

## Why photos are helpful but not essential

Photos of damage or fault codes can speed up an offer. But for many non-runners, reg plus a clear description of the problem is enough for a fair first figure.

## Get a real number, not a guess

If you have been quoted scrap money for a complete supercar, it is worth getting a second opinion from a specialist. The difference is often substantial — and collection plus payment can still happen the same week.
