If your Jaguar has a serious fault, the first thing many owners hear is that the car is "not worth fixing." That is often wrong — and it is almost always wrong when a general buyer is doing the maths.

## 1. Ingenium timing chain rattle

Common on 2.0-litre petrol and diesel Ingenium engines. A rattling chain on start-up is a known issue, and repair costs put many owners off keeping the car.

**What it means for value:** High parts demand for good engines and heads. Your car still has clear salvage and breaker value.

## 2. ZF gearbox failure

Harsh shifts, limp mode or complete failure on six- and eight-speed automatics appear across the range. Main dealers quote eye-watering figures.

**What it means for value:** Gearboxes, transfer boxes and associated modules are sought after. A specialist factors that in.

## 3. Air suspension collapse

XJ, XF and some F-Pace models suffer leaking air struts and compressor failure. The car may sit low on one corner or not rise at all.

**What it means for value:** Individual components and complete axle assemblies hold value even when the system is off the road.

## 4. Electrical gremlins

Infotainment black screens, parking sensor loops and body control module faults are frustrating — but they do not erase the value of the shell, engine and interior.

## 5. Head gasket or coolant loss

Overheating and coolant loss can write a car off in an owner's mind long before it is written off on paper. Again, parts value often exceeds scrap weight by a wide margin.

## The takeaway

A fault that makes your Jaguar unsuitable for daily use does not make it worthless. The key is selling to a buyer who understands what the car is still worth.
