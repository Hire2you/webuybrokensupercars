If your supercar has a serious fault, the first thing many owners hear is that the car is "not worth fixing." That is often wrong — and it is almost always wrong when a general buyer is doing the maths.

## 1. Clutch and flywheel failure

Common on high-performance manual and automated manual gearboxes. A slipping clutch or noisy dual-mass flywheel can mean a repair bill that puts owners off keeping the car.

**What it means for value:** Strong demand for good engines, gearboxes and driveline components. Your car still has clear salvage and breaker value.

## 2. Dual-clutch gearbox failure

Harsh shifts, limp mode or complete failure on PDK, DCT and similar transmissions appear across the range. Main dealers quote eye-watering figures.

**What it means for value:** Gearboxes, differentials and associated modules are sought after. A specialist factors that in.

## 3. Hydraulic or air suspension collapse

Bentley, Ferrari and some Lamborghini models suffer leaking dampers, struts and compressor failure. The car may sit low on one corner or not rise at all.

**What it means for value:** Individual components and complete axle assemblies hold value even when the system is off the road.

## 4. Electrical gremlins

Infotainment black screens, sensor loops and control module faults are frustrating — but they do not erase the value of the shell, engine and interior.

## 5. Head gasket or coolant loss

Overheating and coolant loss can write a car off in an owner's mind long before it is written off on paper. Again, parts value often exceeds scrap weight by a wide margin.

## The takeaway

A fault that makes your supercar unsuitable for daily use does not make it worthless. The key is selling to a buyer who understands what the car is still worth.
