Being told your Porsche might have bore scoring can turn a noise or oil-consumption concern into a much larger decision. Before comparing engine rebuild prices, establish whether there is a confirmed finding and what the proposed work actually covers.

**A suspected fault, a recorded inspection and an agreed repair specification are three different stages.** Knowing which stage you have reached helps you decide whether to investigate further, repair the car or obtain an offer in its present condition.

## What is bore scoring, and what counts as evidence?

Bore scoring refers to damage to a cylinder's internal running surface. A specialist may investigate it when an engine has symptoms such as rising oil consumption, smoke or unusual mechanical noise. These signs can have other causes, so a sound recording or dirty exhaust tip is not enough to confirm it.

[SG Porsche Specialists' explanation](https://www.sgporschespecialists.co.uk/knowledge/porsche-bore-scoring) describes a diagnosis based on the symptoms, mechanical evidence and, where appropriate, a borescope inspection of the cylinder walls. It also explains that inspection images need interpretation alongside the other findings.

Ask for the report and images, the cylinders inspected and the specialist's explanation of what was found. If the wording is 'suspected', preserve that distinction when discussing repair or sale. You do not need to turn an uncertain assessment into a definite diagnosis to request a valuation.

## Identify the engine before comparing advice

The Porsche badge or the words '911 engine rebuild' are not a sufficiently precise specification. Provide the model, year, engine capacity and any previous engine work. If it has a replacement engine, share the available invoice or identifying details.

Much public discussion concerns particular engines and generations. A repair story about one car does not establish the condition or repair requirements of another. Avoid treating an entire model range as affected equally or assuming that an advertised rebuild package applies to your engine.

If you are seeking a second opinion, send the original findings. Ask what can be assessed from those records, what requires another inspection and what each stage costs. The purpose is to clarify the diagnosis and options before committing to major work.

## Why there is no single bore-scoring repair price

The scope may range from further inspection to substantial engine work. A headline rebuild price can leave unanswered questions about removal, refitting, machining, replacement components and what happens when the engine is stripped.

[Hartech's engine guide](https://www.hartech.org/images/downloads/Hartech%20Engine%20Guide%20%28interim%29.pdf) discusses bore damage and the implications of investigation and strip-down for an owner's repair choices. Use specialist material to frame questions about your engine, rather than treating a general guide as a quotation.

Ask the repairer to set out:

- The confirmed damage and the work needed to establish anything still unknown.
- Which cylinders and components the proposed repair covers.
- Engine removal and refitting, fluids, testing and VAT.
- Ancillary work that is recommended or excluded.
- The approval process if the final scope changes.
- The warranty terms and expected completion arrangements.

Clarify whether the number is a fixed quote or an estimate pending dismantling. Two totals are only comparable when you understand what each buys.

## Compare repair with your intended use of the car

If you want to keep the Porsche, the benefit of using it again matters alongside its resale value. A properly understood repair may suit you even when it would not produce a profit on an immediate sale.

If you intend to sell after repair, start with a realistic assessment of that exact car with its repaired-engine history. Subtract the whole repair cost, other work required for sale and any transport or selling costs. Do not use the highest advertised price for a clean running example as guaranteed proceeds.

Compare that result with an actual as-is offer and its conditions. Our [engine-failure repair-or-sell guide](/blog/supercar-engine-failure-repair-or-sell) sets out the wider comparison. If major figures remain unknown, acknowledge the uncertainty rather than filling the gap with an optimistic guess.

## If the engine is already dismantled

An engine out of the car changes the practical handover. Record which components have been removed, where they are held and whether they are included. Photographs and a parts list help a prospective buyer understand what is being assessed.

Ask the workshop about release arrangements and any outstanding agreed charges. Explain whether the Porsche is a rolling vehicle, on a ramp or partly disassembled. A collection plan for a complete car may not cover separate engine components and boxes of parts.

Before authorising reassembly solely for a sale, discuss the present condition with the buyer. Find out what information and transport arrangements are needed instead of assuming more paid work is essential.
