Paperwork is the part most sellers worry about — especially when the car does not drive and you cannot easily visit a post office or drop a buyer at their front door.

## The V5C (log book)

When you sell any vehicle, the V5C must be completed and the change of keeper notified to DVLA. Section 6 (or the online equivalent) records the sale.

If you have lost the V5C, you can apply for a replacement from DVLA. A reputable buyer will guide you through this — it should not block a sale.

## Notification protects you

Until DVLA records the change of keeper, you can remain liable for parking fines, tolls and other matters linked to the registration. That is why you should only sell to a buyer who will complete notification promptly.

We notify DVLA as part of every purchase and can talk you through anything unusual on your V5C before collection.

## SORN and tax

If the car is off the road, it may already be on a Statutory Off Road Notification (SORN). Tax does not transfer with the vehicle — any remaining full months can be reclaimed from DVLA after sale.

## What you need on collection day

Typically:

- V5C (log book)
- Photo ID
- Any spare keys you have
- Service history or receipts if available (helpful but not essential)

## Selling without the stress

Good admin is part of a fair deal. If you are unsure about any document, ask before you agree a price — the right buyer will answer clearly.
